Safety

Safer for everyone on the road, starting with drivers

Safety is the reason for this project. Most of the crashes a road diet prevents are between cars: rear-ends, sideswipes and left turns. The people walking and biking to our schools benefit too.

Why this road crashes

Boston Post Road squeezes four travel lanes into a width that barely fits them. Each lane is about 9.5 feet wide, where the standard is 11. That leaves little room for error.

Then add the turns. Driveways, side streets and parking spaces line the corridor, and every driver waiting to turn left stops in a travel lane. The drivers behind brake hard or swerve into the next lane, and on 9.5-foot lanes both go wrong often.

The evidence keeps getting stronger

Road diets have been studied for decades, and the research has not stood still. The national estimate everyone quotes came from 45 sites. The newest work covers more roads and more recent years, and it separates the crashes that hurt people from the ones that only bend metal. It finds bigger reductions, not smaller.

2014 · Federal Highway Administration

The national guide: 19 to 47% fewer crashes

FHWA gathered the research into one guide and put the headline on page one: converting a four-lane undivided road to three lanes cuts overall crashes by 19 to 47 percent. The range comes from a 2010 FHWA study of 45 sites using the empirical Bayes method, which corrects for changes in traffic and for roads picked right after a bad year. Fifteen small-town sites in Iowa came in at 47%, thirty suburban sites in California and Washington at 19%, and all 45 together at 29%.

2022 · Rhode Island

A Northeastern state with roads like ours, and a significant drop in crashes

Rhode Island’s DOT wanted to know whether road diets were worth adopting across the state, so it had the crashes measured on its own roads — empirical Bayes again, compared against untreated four-lane undivided roads with similar characteristics. Total crashes fell 29%. The crashes that killed or injured someone fell 37%. The roads it studied are the closest match to ours in any of this research: four-lane undivided streets carrying 3,390 to 22,500 vehicles a day, averaging 13,459 — squarely inside the 10,000 to 15,000 Boston Post Road carries.

2022 · Virginia

A large, long-term study, and a significant reduction

Researchers at Virginia's transportation research council measured crashes before and after at 36 stretches of road and 39 intersections converted between 2009 and 2018, by the same method. Total crashes fell by about a third. The crashes that killed or injured someone fell by far more, which is the number that matters on a road where five people have died. Every result was statistically significant, and the roads studied carried 2,600 to 17,000 vehicles a day — the band Boston Post Road sits in.
