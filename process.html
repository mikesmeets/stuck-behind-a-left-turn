Process

Requested locally, paid for by the state

Our municipalities asked for this project. NYSDOT designed it, and state and federal money pays for it. The design isn't final, and there is still time to weigh in.

Where it came from

When NYSDOT planned to repave Boston Post Road, it noticed that downtown Larchmont, from Beach to Alden, has an unusually high number of crashes for its traffic. So it added a Complete Streets safety component to the repaving.

Public outreach

NYSDOT lists the project as "In Development." It has shown alternatives at two public open houses and keeps refining the design based on engineering and public input.

The work behind the plan

NYSDOT has spent the two years since, and hundreds of thousands of dollars, measuring this road. From its own account of the work:

  • Parking inventory, completed July 2024. A count of every signed and striped space, on both sides of Route 1.
  • Pedestrian curb ramp conditions, completed August 2024. Ramp grades and slopes along the entire corridor, the functionality of every pushbutton, sign and signal head, and an ADA compliance assessment.
  • Pavement evaluation, collected September 2024. From the New Rochelle city line to the Town of Rye boundary: reflective cracks, joint separation, longitudinal and edge cracking, raveling, and shoving at intersections.
  • Ground and aerial survey. A detailed ground survey with highway boundary identification and utility mapping from Beach Avenue to Alden Road, finished October 2024, plus control points painted for an aerial mapping flight.
  • Traffic counts and modeling. Turning movement counts at 20 intersections, signal timings, and a model of existing conditions for the full stretch.
  • Crash data. Three years of records, from NYSDOT’s own crash data viewer. The initial review: an “elevated number of crashes compared to similar type corridors.”
  • Design Approval Document. Project need, purpose and objectives; alternatives development and analysis; design standards and criteria; social, economic and environmental conditions; threatened and endangered species and cultural resources; construction impacts.

What came back is a comprehensive Complete Streets redesign built to modern safety standards, with the design work paid for by the state.

What the traffic model is for

Those counts are not collected for their own sake. They feed a model, and NYSDOT’s schedule put the traffic modeling and capacity analysis in December 2024. A capacity analysis is a narrow, well-defined piece of engineering. For every signal on the corridor it asks three questions:

Does the intersection clear?

Whether the green time on each approach is long enough to let through the cars waiting on it, cycle after cycle.

How far back does the line go?

Whether a queue at one light reaches back to the light behind it. That is what turns a slow intersection into a blocked corridor.

How long is the wait?

The average seconds of delay a driver spends at the intersection. That number is what gets graded as Level of Service, A through F.

The model runs the same arithmetic twice, once on the road as it is today and once on the design being proposed, so the two can be compared intersection by intersection. It is the step that answers whether a lane can come out without the traffic backing up — and it happens before a design is put in front of the public, not after.

Who pays

The project is PIN 847315 in the regional transportation plan, where every dollar programmed for it is listed by phase, by year and by funding source.

$20.9M

programmed in total

Federal and New York State transportation funds, from preliminary design through construction.

$0

local contribution

Not one line in the funding table is local money. NYSDOT is running and paying for the project.

10–20%

the usual local match

What most grant-funded road projects ask a town or village to put in. This one asks for nothing.

Where it goes:

  • $16,000,000 for construction, programmed for federal fiscal year 2028.
  • $2,400,000 for construction inspection, the same year.
  • $2,250,000 for design — $1,250,000 of preliminary design already spent, and $1,000,000 of detailed design in 2026.
  • $220,000 for right of way.

The funding sources are worth reading too. Alongside the National Highway Performance Program and New York’s State Dedicated Fund, $4,140,000 of the construction money is federal Highway Safety Improvement Program funding — money that exists to reduce deaths and serious injuries. The plan lists the project’s type as safety, and records that the state certified it as approved by the Regional Safety Evaluation Engineer and signed off by the Regional Traffic Engineer.

The state is rebuilding and restriping the road either way. Doing it as a road diet costs Larchmont and Mamaroneck nothing extra and fixes a known safety problem while the crews are already there.
