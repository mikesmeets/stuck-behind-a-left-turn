Boston Post Road · Larchmont & Mamaroneck

Boston Post Road is dangerous. There's a proven fix.

Anyone who drives, walks or bikes the Post Road knows it isn't safe. In the past 11 years, five people have been killed on it, and 44 pedestrians and 46 cyclists have been hit. Many parents of the 6,000 students at its ten schools won't let their kids walk or bike, so they drive, and the road clogs at drop-off. Our three municipalities unanimously asked NYSDOT to fix it. After two years of study, NYSDOT has proposed a road diet that is safer and keeps traffic moving. It's a proven design, already working in many places, including nearby in Ossining, Rye and Fairfield, Connecticut.

How three lanes can beat four

Boston Post Road's lanes are about 9.5 feet wide, well under the 11-foot standard. On a four-lane road, every driver who stops to turn left blocks a lane. The cars behind either stop too or swerve into the next lane.

A center turn lane gives that driver somewhere to wait. Through traffic keeps moving, and the crashes that come from sudden braking and lane-swerving mostly go away.

Boston Post Road adapts to fit the times

Today's four lanes date from its time as the main route to Boston. They don't fit its job now: a local road that kids use to get to school.

  1. 1670sHorseback and stagecoach

    Colonial riders began carrying mail between New York and Boston in 1673. The coastal route through our towns, the Lower Post Road, carried horses, carts and stagecoaches for two centuries.

  2. 1901–1927Electric trolleys

    Streetcars of the New York and Stamford Railway ran through Larchmont and Mamaroneck, from New Rochelle to Stamford. Buses replaced them by 1927.

  3. 1926US Route 1

    The Post Road became part of US Route 1, the main motor road between New York and Boston. By the 1950s it carried 25,000 cars and “gargantuan interstate trucks” a day through New Rochelle.

  4. 1958I-95 opens

    The New England Thruway opened a short distance away, built to take that load off the Post Road. Today I-95 and the Hutchinson River Parkway carry the long-distance traffic.

  5. TodayA local street

    The Post Road carries local trips now: to school, to the train, to the shops. Daily traffic is down to about 15,000 in Mamaroneck and 10,000 in Larchmont. It still has the four lanes of a highway.

Interstates and parkways connect New York and Boston now. Let's make Boston Post Road a proper local street, one where kids can get to school safely.

Where things stand

The design isn't final. NYSDOT calls the project "In Development" and is still taking public input.

Have a question?

Won't fewer lanes cause gridlock?

NYSDOT's model says no. Our own simulation shows why.

Is the Post Road really that dangerous?

90 pedestrians and cyclists hit in eleven years, clustered around the schools.

What happens to parking?

Most of it stays.

Isn't traffic getting worse?

Not on the Post Road. Daily volumes are at or near their lowest in 45 years.

Who we are: Safe Routes to School

Safe Routes to School is a federal, state and local effort to help children walk and bicycle to school safely. Mamaroneck and Rye Neck Safe Routes to School is a volunteer group of parents. We work with school PTAs and administrators, local police, fire departments and municipal governments, and county, state and federal officials to coordinate safety efforts.

Some of what we do:

Parent surveys

A survey every year on how children get to school and what keeps families from walking or biking. It informs long-term safety plans and the school-travel numbers on this site. 2024–25 results (PDF)

Pedestrian safety education

Pedestrian safety talks for kindergarten, 1st, 5th and 6th grades.

Bike rallies

A spring kickoff to the riding season, with a practice course and bike safety checks and repairs.

Learn to Bike in PE

An eight-session learn-to-bike program in kindergarten PE over the winter.

Bike buses

A weekly Friday ride to school that teaches kids road safety along the way.
