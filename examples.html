Examples

Road diets on streets like ours

A road diet isn't an experiment. State transportation departments have built them on busy main streets for decades, including on state highways that carry more traffic than Boston Post Road.

Woodbury is one of the towns in NJDOT's video on its road diets. See Broad Street on Google Street View.

The closest comparison

Route 45, Woodbury, New Jersey

Broad Street in Woodbury is a state highway through a county seat: a historic downtown, strip centers and blocks of homes, much like Boston Post Road through Larchmont and Mamaroneck. In 2012 NJDOT converted 0.8 miles of it, from Carpenter Street to Red Bank Avenue, from four lanes to one lane each way with a center turn lane and bike lanes, keeping parking where it could.

18,449vehicles a day on Route 45 in Woodbury, 2012
14,958on Boston Post Road in Mamaroneck, 2024; 10,104 in Larchmont

NJDOT made the change to address pedestrian safety and congestion at intersections with no room for left turns. Woodbury's police opposed it at first, worried about response times. After it was built, the retired police chief said it did not affect them.

Nearby examples

US Route 9, Town of Ossining and Briarcliff Manor · 2007

A state highway past strip commercial

South of the Village of Ossining, Route 9 runs past strip shopping and a string of side streets and driveways with no traffic signals. It was converted from four lanes to one lane each way with a center turn lane in 2007, when it carried 15,308 vehicles a day, about what the Post Road carries in Mamaroneck today. No bike lanes were added.

In 2021 a study for the Village of Ossining found that extending the road diet north through the village was feasible.

See it on Google Street View

Commerce Drive, Fairfield, Connecticut

Strip commercial, at Rye Neck's traffic

A strip of shopping centers and restaurants carrying about 10,500 vehicles a day, close to Boston Post Road between Rye Neck and the Rye border (9,740 in 2025). It was converted to one lane each way with a center turn lane.

See it on Google Street View

US Route 1, Fairfield, Connecticut · 2025

The same road, one state over

Connecticut restriped its own Boston Post Road, Route 1 from Pease Avenue to Beaumont Street, from four lanes to three with a center turn lane in 2025, with resurfacing, signal upgrades and curb ramps. It carries about 14,500 vehicles a day, close to the Post Road in Mamaroneck. Street View doesn't show the new layout yet.

Bigger studies

Virginia · Virginia Transportation Research Council, 2020

A statewide look at one state's road diets

Virginia's DOT asked its research council to take stock of every road diet built in the state since 2010. The researchers found 66 projects covering about 39 miles, and asked the cities and counties that built them how they turned out. It's the broadest look at one region's road diets we've found.
