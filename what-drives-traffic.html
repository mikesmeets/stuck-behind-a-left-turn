What drives traffic

Traffic isn't growing. The school run is.

Daily traffic on Boston Post Road is no higher than it was 45 years ago, and on the busiest stretches it has fallen sharply. The crunch people feel comes at drop-off. On a morning with no school, a third of the traffic at one Post Road intersection disappeared.

Why daily traffic fell

More people take the train

As home prices have risen, more residents commute into the city by rail instead of driving.

More people work from home

Fewer commutes every day, especially since the pandemic.

More shopping happens online

E-commerce replaces trips to the store.

The exception: the school run

Schools are the biggest source of traffic and congestion in our area. Every student who is driven makes four car trips a day: drop-off, the drive home, pick-up, and the drive home again.

We measured it. In March 2024 we counted every vehicle through the Post Road intersection at Weaver Street and Hommocks Road, the road to Hommocks Middle School, from 8 to 9 am on two consecutive mornings. One was a normal school day. The other was a superintendent's day, when classes were canceled.

The traffic that vanished was school traffic. Trips in and out of Hommocks Road fell by two-thirds or more, and even through traffic on the Post Road dropped by one car in seven. Ordinary turns to and from Weaver Street barely changed.

Our schools were built expecting children to walk or bike. The streets around them can't absorb every child arriving by car. The road's hardest minutes are the school runs, and every child who can safely walk or bike is one fewer car in that line. Safer walking and biking is exactly what this project builds.
