What drives traffic

Traffic isn't growing. The school run is.

Every stretch of Boston Post Road carries less traffic today than it did in 1990, and on the busiest ones it has fallen sharply. More people take the train, work from home and shop online. But there is still one place a lot of people have to be at the same time every morning: school. That hasn't changed, and it's what drives the Post Road's traffic. On a morning with no school, a third of the traffic at one Post Road intersection disappeared.

Why daily traffic fell

About 8,800 Town of Mamaroneck workers drove to work in 1990. Today about 6,900 do, even though the town has more workers.

The exception: the school run

Schools are the biggest source of traffic and congestion in our area. Every student who is driven makes four car trips a day: drop-off, the drive home, pick-up, and the drive home again.

What we measured here

We measured it. In March 2024 we counted every vehicle through the Post Road intersection at Weaver Street and Hommocks Road, the road to Hommocks Middle School, from 8 to 9 am on two consecutive mornings. One was a normal school day. The other was a superintendent's day, when classes were canceled.

The traffic that vanished was school traffic. Trips in and out of Hommocks Road fell by two-thirds or more, and even through traffic on the Post Road dropped by one car in seven. Ordinary turns to and from Weaver Street barely changed.

The national picture

The same shift has happened across the country, over two generations.

Built for walking

Four of our six district schools were built between 1902 and 1925, in the middle of the neighborhoods they serve. Children were expected to walk or bike to them, and two still have no parking lot at all. Central, built in 1965, has a small lot, and the Hommocks lot wasn't enlarged when that school expanded in the 1990s.

The streets around these schools can't absorb every child arriving by car. The road's hardest minutes are the school runs, and every child who can safely walk or bike is one fewer car in that line. Safer walking and biking is exactly what this project builds.
