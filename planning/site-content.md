# Boston Post Road site — content plan

Working draft. Everything here is meant to be public-safe: no private contact
details, no victims' names, no reporter correspondence.

## Framing

- **Audience:** residents without school-age kids, who are mostly skeptical. Parents
  are largely sold already; for them the site is something to share.
- **Lead with drivers.** Most of the safety benefit is car-on-car crashes
  (rear-end, sideswipe, left-turn) on 9.5 ft lanes. Traffic flow improves.
  No local money. Kids and schools carry weight on the Safety page.
- **The road diet stands on its own.** Ossining and Rye did theirs for safety
  with no bike lanes. The bike lane is a use of freed-up space, not the reason.
- **Tone:** the explanation that was never given. Respectful of NYSDOT: they
  did the analysis, but the public never heard it.
- **Honesty rule carries over:** keep results that don't flatter the case.

## Site map

1. Act, 2. Questions (hub), 3. Safety, 4. Traffic, 5. Parking & business,
6. What drives traffic. Each FAQ answer links to its deep page.

## 2. Questions (hub)

Source: the FAQ posted on social media, carried over with these changes:

- **Where did this come from?** Downtown Larchmont (Beach to Alden) had an
  unusually high crash count for its volume, so NYSDOT added Complete Streets
  to the repaving. In 2024 the three municipalities asked, by unanimous
  resolution, for the full length of Route 1 — largely because of the 10
  schools along it (Chatsworth, Central, Hommocks, MHS, Daniel Warren,
  F.E. Bellows, Rye Neck Middle, Rye Neck High, FASNY Manor, FASNY Harbor),
  with more than 6,000 students.
- **What is a road diet?** Embed the CTDOT Fairfield presentation with chapter

  - Town of Ossining — 2007

  - Fairfield, CT — Commerce Drive (~a decade ago); Route 1 (just finished);

## 3. Safety

- Why here: 9.5 ft lanes vs 11 ft standard; frequent left turns, driveways,
  intersections → weaving, sideswipes, rear-ends behind turning cars.
- FHWA proven safety countermeasure: 19–47% fewer total crashes.
  https://highways.dot.gov/safety/other/road-diets/road-diet-informational-guide/2-why-consider-road-diet
- Local record, Boston Post Road, 2015–2025: 46 cyclists and 44 pedestrians
  hit; 78 injuries, 8 serious, no fatalities. Clustered around the schools
  (especially Hommocks–MHS), both train stations, and Mamaroneck Ave.
  Likely undercounted: uninjured kids get up and keep going.
  NYSDOT counts serious injuries with fatalities as one severity class.
- Map: https://mamaroneck-srts.maps.arcgis.com/apps/mapviewer/index.html?webmap=e79649f1695f4728b057d97eff0ee250
  (permission to embed: _TBD_)
- **Don't wait for the crash.** In 2024 a mother and son were killed at
  Mamaroneck Ave and New Street — not on Boston Post Road — at a signal with
  no exclusive pedestrian phase. The danger was known; changes came after.
  (No names without family consent.)
- **Survey chart slot:** 2025 Mamaroneck SRTS parent survey — ~40% walk,
  ~20% bike. Chart: _data TBD_. Address the obvious question on the page: if
  most kids walk or bike, how are schools the traffic problem? (see page 6)
- Diagrams: braking behind a left-turner and the weave; conflict points 4 vs 3
  lanes; multiple-threat pedestrian crash.

## 6. What drives traffic

- Volumes have declined for years, with a step down after COVID.
  NYSDOT station 870122:
  https://nysdottrafficdata.drakewell.com/sitedashboard.asp?node=NYSDOT_SC&cosit=870122000000
- Chart built: `planning/charts/traffic-trend.html` (AADT, four segments,
  1979–2025, from NYSDOT). Mamaroneck −25% since 2019; Larchmont −24% since
  2014; Rye Neck to Barry −4% since 2019; Rye Neck to Rye border −5% since 2017.
- Why: rising home prices shift commuters to the train; work from home;
  e-commerce replacing shopping trips.
- Meanwhile school drop-off/pick-up peaks have grown as more kids are driven.
  The schools were built for kids who walk; the road network can't absorb
  every child in a car.
- Chart wanted: school day vs summer/holiday day at the same station
  (hourly), if the data exists.
- National trend in walking/biking to school — source from NHTS, _TBD_.
